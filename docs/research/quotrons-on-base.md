# QUOTRONS-style product on Base: research brief

Research date: 2026-08-25. This brief uses first-party product documentation, official Base/Coinbase materials, onchain contract references, the issuer prospectus, and official regulatory statements. It separates confirmed facts from product/legal inference.

## Bottom line

A product inspired by QUOTRONS is technically buildable on Base: a liquid token can materialize/dissolve paired NFTs, an irreversible burn can make an NFT permanent, a fee-taking market can buy Coinbase tokenized stocks, and a reward contract can allocate those stocks to permanent NFTs.

An **exact** clone is neither presently possible nor a sensible starting brief:

1. Base's canonical launch page lists only four live Coinbase Tokenized Stocks—NVDAc, METAc, AAPLc, and GOOGLc. Only NVDA and AAPL overlap QUOTRONS' ten reward tracks. Base's developer page publishes addresses and feeds for thirteen symbols, but nine of those are not yet on the canonical public launch list; they must not be treated as launched merely because an address exists. [Base launch page](https://www.base.org/stocks), [Base integration documentation](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/asset-issuance/tokenized-stocks-on-base)
2. The stock tokens are securities offered under Regulation S to eligible non-US persons. Onchain possession can be permissionless while legal title, voting, and redemption remain conditional on KYC and other “Vesting Conditions”; issuer controls can freeze or otherwise remediate ineligible holdings. A rewards protocol distributing them is therefore not “just another ERC-20 rewards app.” [Coinbase Tokenize FAQ](https://www.coinbase.com/tokenize), [AAPL prospectus](https://assets.ctfassets.net/o10es7wu5gm1/6t7LV7NUfghwRYjZpReFYH/6e08881544b683a4c886aaa809c2d51a/Coinbase_Onchain_SPV_Ltd_-_Prospectus__AAPL__-_FSRA_VERSION.pdf), [SEC staff statement on tokenized securities](https://www.sec.gov/newsroom/speeches-statements/corp-fin-statement-tokenized-securities-012826-statement-tokenized-securities)
3. QUOTRONS' ERC-404/NFT synchronization is a high-risk mechanism, not a visual gimmick. V1 was retired after a stale ERC-721 approval survived a fungible-token-driven ownership change; the project's own record reports 360 transfers into the observed exploit contract. [QUOTRONS exploit mechanics](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-v1-post-mortem/blob/main/docs/01-incident/exploit-mechanics.md)
4. Copying the mechanics does not grant a license to copy the QUOTRONS name, art, copy, or UI. Its official brand kit permits use when talking about QUOTRONS but expressly says not to impersonate the project or imply endorsement; no general software license for the live application was found. [Official QUOTRONS brand kit](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-brand-kit)

The strongest viable thesis is therefore: **an original Base-native collectible whose irreversible state transition earns a curated subset of eligible Coinbase stock tokens from transparent protocol fees**. Whether that product should exist is primarily a compliance, demand, and economic-design question—not a chain-integration question.

## 1. What QUOTRONS actually is

### User-facing loop

QUOTRONS V2 describes itself as a 4,444-terminal ERC-404 collection on Robinhood Chain (chain ID 4663). There is no separate NFT mint sale. One whole liquid QUOTRON materializes a “dark” terminal; dropping below a whole token dissolves a dark terminal back into the available pool. A holder may “hardwire” a chosen dark terminal by irreversibly burning its one-token backing. The result is a permanent ERC-721 with permanently changed “lit” metadata that no longer dissolves with ERC-20 balance changes and can claim stock-token rewards. The stated invariant is `liquid ERC-20 supply + hardwired terminals × 1e18 = 4,444e18`. [QUOTRONS LLM reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/quotrons-llm.md), [complete QUOTRONS reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/llms-full.txt)

The site's main navigation exposes Home, My Desk, Exchange, Social Market, Reward Stocks, OTC Desk, Relics, a V1 Migration Record, and Docs. “My Desk” is the ownership/claim surface; Exchange is the canonical token market; the reward-stock area shows tracks and epoch state; terminal lookup lets a buyer inspect attached pending rewards before purchasing an NFT. Pending rewards are terminal-ID based and travel with a hardwired NFT, although a seller can claim immediately before sale. [Live QUOTRONS application](https://www.quotrons.cash/), [complete QUOTRONS reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/llms-full.txt)

### Verified hybrid-token architecture

V2 is a bespoke ERC-404-style system rather than a single standardized ERC-404 contract. The direct, non-proxy `Quotron404V2` deployment provides the ERC-20 surface and deploys a separate immutable `QuotronMirrorV2` contract providing the ERC-721, metadata, ERC-2981, and ERC721-C creator-token surface. For an ordinary address, the number of dark terminals is kept equal to `floor(ERC-20 balance / 1e18)`; losing a whole-unit boundary dissolves the address's last stored dark ID, while gaining one draws IDs until the invariant is restored. A direct dark-NFT transfer moves exactly one liquid unit with the selected ID, whereas a hardwired-NFT transfer moves no ERC-20 balance. [Verified V2 core](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x5a86828Efd322bfb16d93cFeD16EE9BC14940D7F?tab=contract), [verified V2 mirror](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x027ACa2794E44f24950D81227DcD516FfBB49d6e?tab=contract)

The core's `erc721TransferExempt` list exists for protocol/setup accounts that must hold liquid tokens without materializing dark terminals. Exempt senders and recipients skip the corresponding dissolve/materialize synchronization, and a dark terminal cannot be transferred directly to an exempt recipient. Exemptions can be configured only before launch and only for an account with no dark terminals, so the list is fixed once trading opens. Every base-driven mint, burn, rematerialization, ownership change, recovery, and hardwire clears the token-specific mirror approval; this is the explicit V2 invariant introduced after the V1 stale-approval exploit. [Verified V2 core](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x5a86828Efd322bfb16d93cFeD16EE9BC14940D7F?tab=contract), [verified V2 mirror](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x027ACa2794E44f24950D81227DcD516FfBB49d6e?tab=contract), [V2 security changes](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-v1-post-mortem/blob/main/docs/04-v2/security-changes.md)

### Collection and reward assignment

The 4,440 ordinary terminals have four tiers: 2,450 Quotron I Keypads at 1.0× reward weight, 1,330 Quotron II Desk Units at 1.5×, 530 Quotron 800s at 2.5×, and 130 NASDANK Terminals at 5.0×. The remaining four are one-of-one relics with basket-wide reward carves. Each ordinary terminal is permanently assigned to one of ten tokenized-stock tracks: NVDA, AAPL, TSLA, GME, SPCX, SPY, PLTR, NFLX, RDDT, or MSTR. An ordinary dark terminal earns nothing; a hardwired one earns by live tier weight. Holding a STONK BROKER NFT changes an ordinary terminal's live weight to 1.25×, with public `poke(id)` accounting after owner status changes. [Complete QUOTRONS reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/llms-full.txt)

### Market, fees, and evidenced revenue flows

V2 uses one canonical QUOTRON/WETH Uniswap v4 pool, not ten QUOTRON/stock pools. A dedicated router and hook enforce the supported settlement path. The permanent 3% WETH-side fee is split as follows: 2.0000% buys tokenized stocks for hardwired-terminal rewards, 0.6375% compounds protocol-owned locked liquidity, 0.2125% buys and burns STONKBROKERS, and 0.1500% goes to the creator. Separately, the NFT mirror targets a 5% marketplace creator earning—4% to the keeper and 1% to the creator—when a venue honors its ERC721-C transfer validator. These are evidenced cash-flow rules, not evidence of revenue, profit, or sustainable demand. [QUOTRONS LLM reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/quotrons-llm.md)

The verified V2 seeder is add-only and exposes no liquidity-removal path. Its sole liquidity transaction used the WETH/QUOTRON key with the Uniswap v4 dynamic-fee flag (`0x800000`), tick spacing `60`, range `[-887220, 51600]`, and liquidity `39297763957396791958`. It deposited about 518.55 QUOTRON without a corresponding WETH transfer, creating a one-sided token position; the upper tick implies an opening marginal price of approximately `0.005743 WETH` per QUOTRON. The hook overrides the ordinary LP fee and applies the protocol's WETH-side fee instead. The remaining V2 supply had already been delivered through the V1-to-V2 migration, so the verified transaction establishes the range and opening-price precedent, not how a fresh collection should allocate all 4,444 liquid units. [Verified seeder](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0xCc949C33860E0F695be3373628955D77Dd922Fee?tab=contract), [liquidity transaction](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/tx/0x1041a914945a62e1d633726f77353080ff099e135b845e634013ba3631a2b3e3), [verified WETH hook](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x62E200Cc8e4D95cf622f40Dd70f407C883EcB0cc?tab=contract)

The verified V2 core materialises identities through a lazy Fisher–Yates pool of the currently available IDs. A draw occurs synchronously when a liquid transfer raises the recipient's whole-token balance and hashes `block.prevrandao`, timestamp, block number, recipient, and an incrementing draw nonce. Traits are predetermined by ID and covered by a public assignment hash; there is no commit-reveal or VRF. When a wallet loses a whole unit, its most recently stored dark identity is dissolved and returned to the pool. This is manipulable pseudorandomness rather than cryptographic fairness: a recipient contract can complete a purchase, inspect the result, revert an unwanted draw, and retry later, while the sequencer also has block-selection influence. That surface is materially more important for a fresh launch in which all four valuable special IDs remain discoverable than it was for V2's exact-ID migration distribution. Reproducing the draw for the POC and choosing whether production needs commit-reveal or VRF are separate decisions. [Verified V2 core](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x5a86828Efd322bfb16d93cFeD16EE9BC14940D7F?tab=contract)

The reward executor opens only once at least 0.1 WETH is queued, caps an epoch at 100 WETH, waits at least 60 seconds between epochs, splits WETH equally across the ten tracks, and uses sealed `WETH → USDG → stock` routes. Converted stocks go only to the reflections contract. Per track, 82.5% goes to ordinary hardwired terminals, 12.5% to three basket relics, and 5% to the Gold Indicator. Epoch opening and track conversion are executor-only; the executor chooses timing, per-call conversion amount within the reserved budget, deadline, and minimum outputs, while the owner can pause or replace the executor. Hook-pot pulls are destination-locked to the sealed reflections converter, burn sink, and LP vault. Claims require the caller to own the hardwired ID and pay its pending stocks directly to that current owner; claims remain live even when fresh reflection inflows are paused, and `poke(id)` is public. Rewards therefore queue safely if execution stops, but conversions do not progress until an authorized executor resumes them. [Complete QUOTRONS reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/llms-full.txt), [verified reflections contract](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0xe04fba61FD54Ba78Dd450A30d8Af40167aF5d3Ec?tab=contract), [verified epoch converter](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x24e62Dd5C7058CC41ad9c5375C137460ea1Da2FE?tab=contract), [verified WETH hook](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x62E200Cc8e4D95cf622f40Dd70f407C883EcB0cc?tab=contract)

The verified protocol-owned-liquidity path is specifically **not** a half-swap of queued WETH into QUOTRON. Its LP vault pulls only the queued WETH and adds a one-sided WETH-only v4 position; the callback requires the QUOTRON delta to equal zero, and the contract exposes no removal or withdrawal path. Its executor chooses the tick range, liquidity, maximum WETH input, and deadline, while the owner can pause or replace that executor. The Base project subsequently chose to inherit this one-sided WETH design; that product decision is recorded separately from this QUOTRONS implementation fact. [Verified LP vault](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x4b7A4F53D9b7E4B4941bc9CF74e852D55444cCBe?tab=contract), [verified WETH hook](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x62E200Cc8e4D95cf622f40Dd70f407C883EcB0cc?tab=contract), [project decision](../adr/0001-primary-protocol-economics.md)

Economically, this is a reflexive fee-recycling product: rewards come from trading its own liquid token, not from the earnings of an external business. Hardwiring reduces liquid supply while permanently adding a reward claimant; rewards per terminal dilute as more terminals hardwire, and the source can fall to zero if trading volume disappears. QUOTRONS itself calls the distributions promotional rewards rather than dividends or investment income and disclaims any equity or revenue-share rights. [Live QUOTRONS disclaimer](https://www.quotrons.cash/), [complete QUOTRONS risk disclosure](https://www.quotrons.cash/llms-full.txt)

### Trust and security model

The verified core, mirror, hook, reflections, epoch converter, and LP vault are direct non-proxy deployments with no upgrade function. V2 retains an owner pause/control surface, a blacklist guardian, and a threshold-2-or-greater recovery Safe. The guardian can add freezes but cannot remove them; the recovery Safe can add or remove freezes and forcibly move balances or terminal NFTs between eligible non-protocol accounts, while protected protocol accounts cannot be recovery endpoints. Core pool routing, the mirror transfer validator, reward routes, and fee-pot destinations become sealed; the transfer validator freezes at launch. Metadata URIs become immutable only after the owner separately calls irreversible `freezeMetadata`—the live deployment has done so. Owners nevertheless retain pause and ownership-transfer powers, and the hook/core owner can ban venue codehashes or replace designated executors where the individual contract permits it. Emergency authority therefore remains an explicit trust assumption despite non-upgradeability. [QUOTRONS security reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/quotrons-llm.md), [verified V2 core](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x5a86828Efd322bfb16d93cFeD16EE9BC14940D7F?tab=contract), [verified V2 mirror](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x027ACa2794E44f24950D81227DcD516FfBB49d6e?tab=contract), [verified epoch converter](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x24e62Dd5C7058CC41ad9c5375C137460ea1Da2FE?tab=contract), [verified LP vault](https://robinhoodchain.blockscout.com/address/0x4b7A4F53D9b7E4B4941bc9CF74e852D55444cCBe?tab=contract)

The V1 incident is a direct design warning. When fungible ERC-20 activity dissolved/rematerialized an NFT ID, the mirror did not always clear the previous owner's token-specific ERC-721 approval. A stale approved address could later move the new owner's terminal. V2 says it centralizes every ownership mutation and clears the approval on mirror transfers, fungible-token movement, migration, settlement, and recovery. Any inspired design needs the same invariant tested across every transition, not only `ERC721.transferFrom`. [V1 exploit mechanics](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-v1-post-mortem/blob/main/docs/01-incident/exploit-mechanics.md), [V2 security changes](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-v1-post-mortem/blob/main/docs/04-v2/security-changes.md)

One diligence caveat: QUOTRONS' machine-readable reference says V2 trading and hardwiring are live, while the live homepage can render “V2 staged / trading locked” when its onchain launch reads do not resolve. Its public V2 deployment record also still labels transaction-level attestation and independent-review evidence as pending. Treat production status and audit status as chain-verification questions, not settled facts from the UI. [QUOTRONS reference](https://www.quotrons.cash/quotrons-llm.md), [public V2 deployment record](https://github.com/mavrkofficial/quotrons-v1-post-mortem/blob/main/docs/04-v2/deployment-and-distribution.md)

### B20 behavior that matters to a rewards app

B20 is an ERC-20-compatible native precompile standard rather than ordinary per-token EVM bytecode. It adds roles, policy scopes, seizure, granular pause features, supply caps, mutable metadata, memo transfers, and an asset multiplier. Standard ERC-20 selectors/events remain compatible, but integrations must not assume ordinary immutable ERC-20 semantics. [B20 specification](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/specs/reference/b20)

For stock tokens, a WAD-scaled multiplier represents corporate actions. One raw B20 token does not permanently equal one share; dividends are currently reinvested into underlying shares after applicable withholding/fees and reflected by changing the multiplier, as are stock splits. A rewards UI must use scaled balances and current multiplier data rather than presenting raw token count as share count. [Base tokenized-stock guide](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/asset-issuance/tokenized-stocks-on-base), [Coinbase Tokenize FAQ](https://www.coinbase.com/tokenize)

Chainlink's launch feeds are 24/5 total-return feeds, not the token's 24/7 DEX price. They hold the last close outside supported market hours and freeze during corporate actions. Base explicitly instructs consumers to check `updatedAt`, apply staleness bounds, and never settle or liquidate against a frozen feed. A reward converter should execute against actual DEX quotes with strict minimum-out protection; a Chainlink feed can be a guardrail, but not a blind weekend execution price. [Base price-feed documentation](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/asset-issuance/tokenized-stocks-on-base#price-feeds)

### Eligibility is more nuanced than “permissionless”

Base says secondary holding and trading are permissionless and KYC is limited to Authorized Participants during mint/redeem, while policies can block sanctioned addresses. Coinbase says the products are offered under Regulation S and unavailable to US persons and other restricted jurisdictions. [Base compliance section](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/asset-issuance/tokenized-stocks-on-base#compliance), [Coinbase Tokenize FAQ](https://www.coinbase.com/tokenize)

The prospectus supplies the critical nuance: an unvested onchain holder can have beneficial economic exposure, but only a Vested Holder can exercise voting and redemption rights. Vesting can require KYC/AML and jurisdiction checks; it can later be suspended or revoked; tokens at ineligible addresses can be frozen, cancelled, or otherwise remediated. DeFi transfers must still comply with applicable law, even if the smart contract permits a transfer. [AAPL prospectus](https://assets.ctfassets.net/o10es7wu5gm1/6t7LV7NUfghwRYjZpReFYH/6e08881544b683a4c886aaa809c2d51a/Coinbase_Onchain_SPV_Ltd_-_Prospectus__AAPL__-_FSRA_VERSION.pdf)

This does not decide any project's status in every jurisdiction, but it defeats the premise that calling payouts “promotional rewards” removes securities-law exposure. ## 4. Key risks and unresolved facts

1. **Distribution authority and eligible recipients.** Does Coinbase/its tokenization entity permit an unaffiliated protocol to acquire these securities from a DEX and distribute them algorithmically to pseudonymous NFT holders? The public materials show DeFi composability but do not specifically approve this rewards model. Written issuer confirmation and jurisdiction-specific counsel are launch gates.
2. **Target jurisdiction.** “Non-US” is not a complete eligibility policy. The prospectus can impose additional jurisdiction, sanctions, and vesting conditions. The team must name where the operator is incorporated, where founders work, and where the frontend/protocol will solicit users before architecture or marketing is fixed.
3. **Available stock universe.** The canonical launch list is four, not thirteen. There is no evidence yet that the other nine developer-documented assets have a live prospectus, sufficient transferable supply, or deep executable liquidity.
4. **Liquidity and conversion loss.** “Deep liquidity” is a launch claim for Aerodrome, not a guarantee at the epoch size the app will need. Each route needs live pool addresses, depth, price impact, stale-oracle handling, and a failure policy. A ten-way equal split can waste value on the thinnest route.
5. **Issuer/admin risk.** B20 supports pause, policy, seizure, metadata changes, and immediate admin actions without a standard-level timelock. The issuer—not Base—controls token configuration. Rewards may become frozen, untransferable, or nonredeemable at the recipient wallet. [B20 specification](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/specs/reference/b20), [Base disclaimer](https://docs.base.org/base-chain/asset-issuance/tokenized-stocks-on-base#disclaimer)
6. **Hybrid-token security.** ERC-20 and ERC-721 ownership transitions share state. Every transfer, materialization, dissolution, hardwire, recovery, pool settlement, and marketplace path must clear or preserve approvals correctly. QUOTRONS V1 proves this is an exploit class, not a theoretical concern.
7. **Keeper and route governance.** Who can open epochs, update routes, pause conversions, rescue unrelated assets, and respond to a stock pause? Destination locks help, but a dead keeper still halts payouts.
8. **Reflexive economics.** A 3% transfer/trading toll is high, and two-thirds of it recycles into rewards. The product needs organic reasons to trade despite the toll. Otherwise “yield” is simply the participants' own trading cost redistributed to a shrinking liquid float and growing permanent claimant base.
9. **Marketing and product classification.** A collectible marketed by expected stock rewards may be analyzed differently from art marketed for use or enjoyment. Disclaimers do not control the economic substance. Avoid “dividend,” “passive income,” “real yield,” or return promises until counsel approves exact language.
10. **IP and differentiation.** “QUOTRONS on Base” risks looking like an unauthorized fork or affiliation. An original theme, collection logic, name, artwork, and copy are necessary even if the state machine is inspired by QUOTRONS.

## 5. First grilling decision tree

These questions should be answered in order; each answer changes the viable product.

1. **What does “exactly like” mean?** Is the goal the economic state machine, the retro-terminal collectible experience, the precise ten-stock distribution, or the visual brand? Recommended answer: copy only the high-level state-machine insight and create an original identity.
2. **Who may use it?** Name the operator's jurisdiction, founder locations, frontend-served jurisdictions, prohibited jurisdictions, and whether wallet-level eligibility will be enforced. If the intended market includes US persons, pause the stock-reward design before writing contracts.
3. **What is the user's actual job-to-be-done?** Collecting evolving art, speculating on a scarce protocol token, receiving diversified stock exposure, or playing a burn/commitment game? If “get stock exposure” wins, directly holding the B20 stocks is simpler and cheaper than paying a 3% wrapper toll.
4. **What creates durable demand for the liquid token?** Rewards alone are circular because they are funded by its own trading. State the non-yield reason somebody repeatedly buys or trades.
5. **Must rewards be those exact ten names?** If yes, the product is blocked today. If no, choose between the four canonical launch assets or an even smaller two-stock MVP using the exact overlap with QUOTRONS (AAPLc and NVDAc).
6. **Is stock assignment random, chosen, or portfolio-weighted?** Random permanent assignment creates collectible scarcity but can saddle a user with unwanted securities exposure. Choice weakens rarity but improves intent and suitability.
7. **How does a recipient become eligible?** Decide whether the app checks B20 policy state only, uses an eligibility/KYC provider, gates claims, or lets rewards accrue until a compliant destination is supplied. Do not assume a successful ERC-20 transfer means the recipient has full legal rights.
8. **Where does conversion execute?** Identify canonical Aerodrome pools/routers or an aggregator, stock-by-stock depth, maximum epoch size, slippage caps, Chainlink deviation guardrails, and what happens during off-hours/corporate-action feed freezes.
9. **Which powers remain administrative?** Specify pause, route update, keeper replacement, sanctions response, recovery, metadata, and treasury powers; choose multisig thresholds and timelocks where compatible with incident response.
10. **What is the smallest defensible experiment?** Recommended technical scope after legal clearance: an original identity, testnet-first hybrid token/NFT state machine, two reward stocks, one conversion route per stock, no relics/boost NFT/OTC desk/social market at launch, and exhaustive invariant/fuzz testing before an audited mainnet deployment.

### Hard gates before a mainnet build

- A written legal memo covering the operator and target-user jurisdictions, distribution/marketing of Coinbase Tokenized Stocks, sanctions/KYC/vesting, and treatment of the project's own fee-bearing token and NFTs.
- Written confirmation from the issuer/integration team that algorithmic reward distribution is an intended/supported DeFi use, plus a definitive eligible-jurisdiction and recipient-control integration path.
- Verified public-launch status, prospectus, pool addresses, executable liquidity, policy configuration, multiplier, and Chainlink feed for every enabled stock.
- A mechanism/economics simulation covering fee elasticity, hardwire rate, reward dilution, keeper costs, conversion slippage, creator revenue, and zero/low-volume failure modes.
- Independent security review of the ERC-20/ERC-721 ownership and approval state machine, plus invariant, fuzz, fork, and recovery-path tests modeled directly on the QUOTRONS V1 failure.
